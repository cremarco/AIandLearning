EVIDENCE CARDS — ENGLISH
Original short paraphrases of checked sources; no raw abstracts. Checked 31 October 2026.

Access: use screen readers, text-to-speech, dictation, translation and approved accommodations. Discuss orally or in writing; no account or app is required.

A01 — Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics
In a classroom-randomized mathematics study, unrestricted GPT-4 improved assisted practice but reduced performance on an immediate unassisted exam. Guardrailed tutoring avoided the observed exam loss without establishing an independent-learning gain. Reported percentages are relative changes, not percentage points.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122

A02 — AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: an RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting
An RCT with 194 Harvard students compared a designed AI tutor with active-classroom physics lessons. Immediate posttests favored tutoring; this compared instructional packages. The 316 posttest observations do not represent 316 participants, and long-term retention was not established.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6

A03 — Beware of metacognitive laziness: Effects of generative artificial intelligence on learning motivation, processes, and performance
A randomized writing study with 117 university students found better ChatGPT-assisted essays, without significant advantages in knowledge, transfer or intrinsic motivation. Self-regulation processes differed. One laboratory task and small groups limit generalization; “metacognitive laziness” is an interpretation, not a diagnosis.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13544

A06 — Enhancing School Students’ Self-Regulated Learning through Generative AI Support: A Randomized Controlled Trial
A preregistered RCT with 371 school students compared two prompting approaches with standard ChatGPT across six lessons. It found no significant advantage in the main outcomes. The comparator also used AI: this does not show AI equals no AI. Knowledge assessment was brief.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10133-8

FACILITATOR NOTES
Match each card to: study design; participants/task; outcome measured; finding; limitation. Distinguish an assisted product, an immediate independent test, and delayed retention. When discussing independent reasoning, keep all access supports and approved accommodations available.

Deliberately excessive claim to revise:
“AI tutors improve learning for every student in every subject.”

Ask: Which source supports which narrower claim? What remains untested? What would we measure next? A favorable or unfavorable conclusion is not required; a precise, supported one is.

Checks: A01 percentages are relative changes, not percentage points. A02 has 194 participants. A03 concerns writing in a specific laboratory task. A06 compares prompting approaches with standard ChatGPT, not no AI.
