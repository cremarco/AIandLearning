Content / steps (linear reading order):

2. Independent content check: What can the learner explain or do?
3. Retention: What remains after an interval?

Card 1
title: Assisted product
body: Check the quality of the answer while help is available, such as an AI-assisted essay.
Card 2
title: Independent check

Card 3
title: Retention
body: Check what the learner remembers after an interval, beyond the immediate end of the activity.

title: Transfer
body: Ask the learner to use the idea in a different task or case. Check the explanation as well.

SLIDE S09 — Better practice can hide a weaker independent test
Page 10 / 44

University: 1.5 min; 09:00–10:30; position 10/34
Teachers: 2 min; 12:00–14:00; position 12/33

Message: In one school mathematics RCT, unrestricted AI improved practice but reduced unaided exam performance.

Content / steps (linear reading order):
1. About 1,000 pupils · one Turkish school · four sessions
2. GPT Base: practice +48%; unaided exam −17%
3. GPT Tutor: practice +127%; no significant unaided exam advantage
4. All percentages are relative to the control group

Study / context: Classroom RCT · about 1,000 pupils · one Turkish school · four sessions

Observed in this study:
+48% — GPT Base · assisted practice
−17% — GPT Base · unaided exam

Result note: Relative changes versus control, not percentage points

Finding: GPT Tutor: +127% in practice; no significant unaided exam advantage.

Limit: Immediate outcomes in one setting; long-term retention was not tested.

Additional limits / guardrails from the deck data:
- Classroom RCT; a single site.
- Immediate exam, not long-term retention.
- No significant advantage is not proof of universal equivalence.

Sources:

Message: Different tutors, tasks, controls and measures can produce different outcomes.

Content / steps (linear reading order):
