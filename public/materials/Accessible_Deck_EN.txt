SLIDE S52 — Andrea’s learning task
Page 10 / 99
To understand the chapter and show what he has learned, Andrea must:
1. Read
Study the chapter. Identify the key ideas and what is still unclear.
2. Represent
Build a diagram with labelled links between the concepts.
3. Check
Compare the diagram and AI suggestions with the chapter. Correct errors.
4. Explain
Use his own words to explain a new example, without AI help.
Evidence: a checked diagram, an independent explanation and a justified correction.

SLIDE S52SDL — Self-directed learning: making choices
Page 11 / 99
Andrea takes an active role: he identifies his learning needs, chooses a path and reviews his progress.
1. Identify a goal
What do I need to learn, and what do I want to achieve?
2. Choose a path
Which resources, people and strategies will help me learn?
3. Review progress
What can I explain now, and what needs more work? Self-assessment helps here.
Andrea’s example: “I need to distinguish training from inference. I will use the chapter and a concept map, then check whether I can explain the difference.”
Andrea takes responsibility for his choices, with support from teachers and peers.
[EDU_SELF_DIRECTED] National Library of Medicine. (n.d.). Self-Directed Learning as Topic (MeSH D000073819). Definition attributed to Knowles (1975), Self-directed learning: A guide for learners and teachers. New York: Association Press.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/2023167
Conceptual definition, not an efficacy trial. Knowles’s definition was verified through the NLM entry; the full original book was not consulted. The three groups and Andrea’s example are workshop illustrations.

SLIDE S52SA — Self-assessment: checking progress
Page 12 / 99
Andrea judges his progress against clear criteria, then uses that judgement to improve his work.
1. Check the criteria
What should I be able to explain or do?
2. Look for evidence
What can I demonstrate? Where do I still struggle?
3. Choose a next step
What will I revise, practise or check again?
Andrea’s example: “I can explain what a token is, but I still confuse training and inference. I will revise that link in my map and explain it again.”
AI can offer feedback; the learner checks the evidence and decides what to improve.
[EDU_SELF_ASSESSMENT] Andrade, H. L. (2019). A critical review of research on student self-assessment. Frontiers in Education, 4, 87.
https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2019.00087
Definition and review of 76 empirical studies; not validation of this workshop, its three-step illustration or AI feedback. Andrea’s example is a constructed teaching scenario.

SLIDE S52PHASES — A study process in six phases
Page 13 / 99
Choose support for your next learning action. The corner ribbon shows the main phase, with a smaller secondary phase.
1. PLAN
Set your goal, identify your starting point and organise tasks and time.
2. UNDERSTAND
Clarify difficult ideas through explanations, examples and questions.
3. CONNECT
Explain ideas in your own words and organise relationships between concepts.
4. RECALL
Retrieve what you know without looking, then revisit it over time.
5. APPLY
Use what you have learned to solve problems and handle new situations.
6. CHECK
Compare your performance with criteria, identify gaps and decide what to study next.
Checking guides your next step. SUPPORT marks setup, sources and sharing.
